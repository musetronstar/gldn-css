Three font pairings

Each pairing sets sans, serif and mono on the same golden scale. Headings use the sans by default; each card also shows a serif heading. The quotations are from Vannevar Bush, “As We May Think,” The Atlantic Monthly, July 1945.

Selected: pairing B. gldn-fonts.css bundles Inter, Literata and JetBrains Mono for pages that link it; without the sheet, pages use the system fonts of pairing A. Pairing C stays for comparison.

Light theme

A. System fonts

The default: fonts that ship with each operating system, so nothing downloads. The serif is Palatino where the system has it.

Sans for interface and prose

A memex is a device in which an individual stores all his books, records, and communications, and which is mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility. It is an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory.

Similar shapes stay apart: Il1, O0, rn and m; figures 3/8 and 1,618; bold, italic and italic inline code.

Serif for long reading

Consider a future device for individual use, which is a sort of mechanized private file and library. It needs a name, and, to coin one at random, "memex" will do.

In the serif, italic and bold: Il1, O0, 3/8 and 1,618.

Wholly new forms of encyclopedias will appear, ready made with a mesh of associative trails running through them, ready to be dropped into the memex and there amplified.

Vannevar Bush, “As We May Think,” The Atlantic Monthly, July 1945
